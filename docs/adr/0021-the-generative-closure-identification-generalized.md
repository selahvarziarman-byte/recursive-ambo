# 0021 — The generative closure: identification generalized, the enactment and signed-class laws, the two-sided mode law, and forms that beget with no last generation

Status: **Accepted** — researcher-defined, mothership-ratified (by independent re-derivation of every seal), engineer-sealed + coder-built-blind + audited, committed on `team-arman` (Arman native, 2026-07-10 → 07-12). Consolidates the connect-sum arc, the general complex-identification arc, the acquisition chain, and the registry unbounding. Supersedes nothing; completes **ADR 0008** (the module is a generative playground) and **ADR 0009** (genealogy is the standing object).

## Context

ADR 0008 promised a **generative playground**: forms beget forms. **The promise did not hold**, and the grounding showed exactly why.

Every *identifying* operation we had (`glue`, `flip-glue`, `collapse`) was a **fundamental-polygon** operation, bound to a single polygon at every level of its machinery: pairings addressed by polygon **slot index**; edge classes a union-find over slots; the vertex link built from **corner positions in the polygon cycle**; `SurfaceTrace` hardcoding `cellCounts.f = 1` ("the single polygon"); `materializeSurfaceResult` emitting **exactly one face**. The Q-M2 chaining unfreeze generalized this only *within* the polygon world — a longer word on the same polygon, replayed from the birth word — which is why it had to "recover the birth word," and why it refused when there was no word (assemble-born, dual-born).

**Consequently a born form could not be operated on.** A `connectedSum` genus-2 (30 faces) had no polygon, so the word ops could not address it — and a face-discarding lie was live: their `canApply` checked only the *selected face's* edge count, never `form.faces.length`, so firing "glue" on a 30-face form returned a single-quad fragment and **silently dropped 29 faces**.

This ADR records the arc that closed the gap — and the laws discovered inside it, several of which corrected the offices that wrote them.

## Decision

### 1 · "Combine two forms" = the CONNECTED SUM — a MACRO, not a primitive
`connectedSum(M₁, M₂, mode)` = `cutCell(M₁, diskA)` + `cutCell(M₂, diskB)` + the **enacted** `assemble` identifying the two rims. No new primitive: every step is a committed op.
- **CUT is the INVERSE of GLUE.** Glue binds/closes (identify → reduce boundary); cut opens/severs (remove → create boundary, a **recorded loss**). **Handle = a passage; genus = the number of independent passages.** Connected sum = open a mouth in each form, bind the mouths.
- **The disk carries NO topological information** — it is the contractible piece, and *that is why excising it is faithful*. Cut information-rich material and you destroy a real cycle. The **wound-locus is craft** (a representative choice), never result-bearing.
- **The seam is SEPARATING ⇒ null-homologous** — invisible to the drawn H₁ (`[C] = 0`); the connect adds **no generator**. The information is in **what you attach** (the second form's own generators) and in **the mode**.
- **Dyck:** a handle stays a clean passage only on an **orientable** base; on a non-orientable form it dissolves into crosscaps (`T²#N_k ≅ N_{k+2}`). Genus adds only on an orientable base.

### 2 · The general COMPLEX-IDENTIFICATION op — `identify(complex, cycleA, cycleB, modes)`
Identify two matched **edge-class walks** (cycles or arcs) of **one** complex, **enacted complex-wide**, and let the committed gate judge. This is the **arity-1 SELF sibling of `connectedSum`** (which is the two-form case). **Modes are per-pair**, not per-op (forced: Klein's word `abaB` is mixed).
- **BOUNDARY-identification is the meaningful sub-family** (free edges → sound manifolds: a cylinder's two rims → torus/Klein). **Interior identification is ADMITTED, not pre-refused** — it merely pinches, and the **gate** refuses the result, naming the junction (*instruments, not guards* — ADR 0004/0006).
- **The polygon word-ops are special cases of this op** — a polygon is a one-face complex; a word is a boundary-edge identification on it. **And the op realizes that by DELEGATION, not by re-derivation:** on a first-generation single-face form, `identify()` hands off to the committed word machinery verbatim (`glueFace` / `flipGlueFace` + `materializeSurfaceResult`) — *reuse, never fork*. The five words therefore come out **byte-identical by construction**, and the five-word byte-compare witnesses the **adapter** (edge-walk → slot resolution + the mode convention) — **NOT the general enactment**, which it never executes.
  > ⚠ **CORRECTION (2026-07-12).** This ADR originally claimed the five words "fall out of the general enactment, proven not argued." **That was false.** The sealed source says plainly that on the shared domain the two "**ARE the same code**," and the diagnostic asserts `via === 'committed-word'`. The code never misrepresented this; **two senior offices (engineer and mothership) read a construction and reported it as a discovery.**
  >
  > ✅ **RESOLVED the same day.** The witness the claim pretended to be **now actually exists** — the **differential oracle** (Consequences, below): an independently-constructed reference quotient that **executes** `identifyOnComplex` and agrees with it **byte-for-byte** (edge classes, vertex classes, signed slots, shape cycles, ledger membership, gate sets) at every generation of the depth-4 chain, catches **five distinct injected defects**, and reproduces the cylinder seal and the depth-4 surface **on its own**. The general enactment is now **known** honest, not believed honest. The five-word compare keeps its true and narrower job: it witnesses the **adapter**.

### 3 · IDENTIFY MEANS MERGE (path-1)
`A ≡ B` means A and B **become one edge-class**, carrying the union of their wedges. Free edges: merge → a 2-wedge manifold edge (the meaningful case). Interior edges: merge → a 4-wedge junction (the gate refuses). **Merging endpoints while keeping two classes (path-2) is NOT edge-identification** — it is vertex-identification, a distinct and weaker op. The op must **explicitly merge the declared classes**.

### 4 · THE ENACTMENT LAW (D3) — the returned shape must BE the quotient
An identifying/assembling op must **materialize the quotient** (rewrite every edge endpoint and face cycle through `resultOf`, de-duplicate the identified classes), **not merely record the ledger**.
**Why this is not a nicety:** **χ, F and b₁ are BLIND to the union-vs-quotient defect** — for `T²#T²` both read −2 / 30 / 4. Only **b₀ (connectivity), boundary, b₂ and w₁** separate the disconnected, bounded pre-glue union from the true quotient. A ledger-only `assemble` therefore renders **the wrong complex**, and **χ never betrays it**. The ledger, genealogy and gate are untouched by the enactment; only the *structure* is enacted.

### 5 · THE SIGNED-CLASS LAW — explicit signed edge classes, never endpoint-derived
The enactment must carry each edge's **explicit class identity and sign** through the quotient and merge **only the declared classes**. Re-deriving classes from rewritten **endpoints** fuses **parallel classes** and **corrupts χ** (grounded: the cylinder-rim sew reads χ = 3 / χ = 1 and non-manifold instead of χ = 0). This is `materializeOperation`'s route-B trap, and it applies in full to every general op.

### 6 · THE MODE — its DEFINITION (the reference), then its LAW

**6.0 · The mode's REFERENCE** — ⚠ **SUSPENDED 2026-07-12, SAME DAY: the rule below is GAUGE-DEPENDENT and its polarity is INVERTED for the engine's storage. Do not build to it.** *(Correction in progress; the corrected rule is stated in 6.0-bis. Retained here only so the error is legible.)*

> **MEASURED (mothership, independent):** the polarity of an aligned/anti-aligned rule **inverts with the edge-storage convention.** With `s_X` = the sign with which X's single incident face traverses X's *stored* arrow: on a **canonical-grid** storage (`s_A·s_B = −1`) **ALIGNED** → torus; on the engine's **face-order** storage (`s_A·s_B = +1`, edges minted from face cycles) **ANTI-ALIGNED** → torus. **Neither fixed sign is invariant.** The invariant rule is **σ = −s_A·s_B** — which reproduces the sealed torus under **both** storages.
> **My own error, precisely:** I "independently re-derived" the pin on **my own** storage convention, under which it happens to be true. Independent of the code — **but not of my own assumption.** The seal (torus) is the invariant truth; the alignment *label* is gauge, and I ratified a gauge-dependent rule as if it were absolute.

**6.0-bis · The SETTLED reference — THE CANONICAL WEDGE** *(researcher-pinned; built + committed `ef704d0`, "the canonical wedge — `identifyOnComplex` is a function of its own input again")*

> **THE RULE (all valences).** `σ = −s_A·s_B`, where `s_X` is read from **X's CANONICAL WEDGE** — the incident wedge lying on the face of **smallest committed face-id**. **PRESERVING = ORIENTATION-COMPATIBLE** (the two faces traverse the merged edge in opposite senses). At valence 1 the canonical wedge *is* the only wedge, so this **reduces exactly** to the free-edge rule below — the valence-1 reduction is **byte-exact** (engineer-verified: rims both modes at res 4/5/6/8/12, the parallel-class tubes, and the depth-4 chain at every generation — **zero divergence**).
>
> **SAME-FACE TIEBREAK** *(researcher, ruled)* — when **both wedges lie on ONE face**, "smallest face-id" names a face, not a wedge. From each slot citing the edge, read the face's boundary word **cyclically, recording COMMITTED EDGE-IDS ONLY** (directions dropped — they are not re-storage-invariant; edge-ids are). Choose the **lexicographically smallest** read-out. *(Relabelling-invariant: the read-out starts at the slot and never consults `complex.faces` order. Re-storage-invariant: edge-ids do not change under an arrow flip.)*
>
> **⚠ THE TIE IS A FORCED REFUSAL — a THEOREM, not a gap.** If the read-outs are **equal**, the two wedges are exchanged by a **cyclic symmetry of the face's boundary word** (canonically: the torus word `a b a⁻¹ b⁻¹` → edge-ids `a b a b`, where both `a`-slots read `[a,b,a,b]`). The only remaining data are **direction** (fails re-storage) and **position** (fails relabelling). **No selector satisfying both standing invariance clauses can exist**, and an arbitrary choice would yield objects that are isomorphic but **not byte-identical** — which the byte-exact clauses forbid. **The engine REFUSES, naming the symmetry.** *A tie is the engine naming a symmetry of the form: the choice is undefined, not unruled.* The refusal is a **positive statement about the object**.
>
> **Meaning (so no future reader mis-imports it):** the interior/same-face case is a sub-case of valence 2 — both wedges traverse the edge oppositely, so orientation-compatibility is **vacuous** there. It carries **no orientation semantics**; it is a **naming** over a gate-recorded junction (ADR 0006). Hence *where a tiebreak exists it is free* (any invariant selector suffices) — **but the degeneracy itself carries meaning: not orientation, SYMMETRY.**

*(Deferred, scope — mothership: the read-out is **word-local**. A tie proves the *face's word* is symmetric; a complex-**global** canonical labelling could break some ties the word-local rule cannot. **Deferred** (researcher's recommendation, accepted): non-blocking, and where the symmetry is a genuine automorphism of the complex — the torus polygon is one — **no selector exists at any strength and the refusal is permanent**.)*

**6.0-ter · The road here (kept legible — three offices, three gauge errors)**
- **The mode's MEANING is fixed by the seal and is not a matter of convention: PRESERVING = ORIENTATION-COMPATIBLE** — the two faces traverse the merged edge in **opposite** senses (as the two wedges of any interior edge of an oriented surface already do).
- **FREE (1-wedge) edges — DERIVED, not chosen:** `σ = −s_A·s_B`. A free edge has exactly **one** wedge, so `s` is unambiguous, **invariant under face-array order**, and **invariant under re-storage** (flip a stored arrow and `s` flips with it, selecting the same physical pairing). This reproduces the seal under every storage. **The shipped enactment already implements this on free edges — which is why no sealed surface ever moved.**
  **PROVEN invariant (measured twice, independently — mothership and engineer).** Across four gauges (identity · a **partial** flip splitting an identified pair · a uniform flip of all free edges · a uniform flip of every edge), `σ = −s_A·s_B` yields the sealed torus in **all four**. Both fixed-sign rules fail: *aligned* is wrong at every gauge on the engine's storage; ***anti-aligned* survives the uniform gauges and dies only on the PARTIAL one** (χ=−2, w₁=1). **⚠ Hence a re-storage test is only a witness if the flipped subset is PROPER and NON-UNIFORM — a uniform flip moves `s_A` and `s_B` together, leaves `s_A·s_B` fixed, and lets the wrong rule pass.**
- **INTERIOR (2-wedge) edges:** the two wedges disagree, so orientation-compatibility is **vacuous** at this valence — the mode there is a **naming**, not a physical distinction. It is settled by the **canonical wedge** (§6.0-bis), which is invariant under both relabelling and re-storage. **⚠ Three offices proposed three wrong references before this one held:** the mothership's *stored-orientation pairing* (gauge-dependent — its polarity inverts with the storage), the engineer's *fixed anti-aligned* (gauge-dependent — survives a uniform re-storage, dies on a partial one), and the shipped *array-order* wedge (relabelling-dependent — `identifyOnComplex` was not a function of its own input). **The rule that survived is the one none of them proposed, and the code had been running its valence-1 case correctly since day one.**

**★ THE TWO INVARIANCE CLAUSES ARE INDEPENDENT — neither may ever be retired as duplicative** *(engineer, measured cell-by-cell in `diagnose-canonical-wedge` §[e]; canonized here so no future refactor can drop one)*:

| mechanism | **relabelling** test | **re-storage** test |
|---|---|---|
| array-order `s` (the killed bug) | **CAUGHT** | **passes** |
| raw fixed-sign (the suspended pin) | **passes** | **CAUGHT** |
| **canonical wedge** (the rule) | invariant | invariant |

> **Re-storage is BLIND to the array-order bug** (`σ = −s_A·s_B` is gauge-invariant *whichever* wedge you reference, so long as you reference **one**). **Relabelling is BLIND to the fixed-sign bug** (it never reads a wedge at all). **A seal carrying only ONE of them passes a wrong mechanism.** This is the *parametric-seal* clause firing one level up — not at a clause's parameter, but at **the clause SET**.

**Why this had to be pinned — the choice is load-bearing (independently re-derived on a 4-triangle strip):**
- **Shared-endpoint edges:** preserving → V=4, **χ=0**; reversing → V=5, **χ=1**. *The choice moves χ.*
- **Disjoint-endpoint edges:** both give V=4, E=8, F=4, **χ=0** — **χ is BLIND** — and only the **vertex partition / degree sequence** separates them (`[2,2,6,6]` vs `[2,2,5,7]`). **Two different objects that χ cannot tell apart.** This is the §4 pattern again, one level down: *the generic case is the one the crude invariant cannot see* — which is precisely why the reference must be pinned in the canon and witnessed in the byte-structure, not left to the implementation.

**Gauge note (honest).** The `preserving`/`reversing` **label** is relative to the committed stored orientations — a re-storage of the same complex would swap the labels, as orientation-labelling always is. But **the committed complex fixes the storage**, so the mode is well-defined on it, and **the effect (which object is born) is determined absolutely**. The free/boundary case removes even the label-gauge (the single wedge canonicalizes it) — which is why the meaningful sub-family carries an unambiguous torus-vs-Klein reading and the interior case does not.

**6.1 · THE MODE LAW — two-sided, and its inertness is LEVEL-2-SCOPED**
> **The mode BITES ⟺ the identified seam is NON-SEPARATING. The mode is INERT ⟺ the seam is SEPARATING.**
> **Operational test: remove the seam curve and count components — 2 ⇒ inert; 1 ⇒ load-bearing.**

**Both sides are measured, not argued.** *Inert:* the `connectedSum` cross-glue seam is separating **by construction** (the interface of two previously-disjoint pieces) — both modes give the orientable genus-2. *Biting:* the self-identification of one form's two rims is non-separating — preserving → **torus** (w₁=0), reversing → **Klein** (w₁=1). It still bites at generation four (genus-2 vs N₄).

**Crosscaps enter ONLY from a non-orientable INPUT** (Dyck: `T²#RP² → N₃`) **or a self-gluing reversal** (`flipGlue` — a non-separating, 1-sided-capable identification) — **never** from the connect-sum seam direction. *(Both the mothership charter and the researcher's own ruling once claimed otherwise; both offices owned the error. A separating curve is null-homologous, hence 2-sided, hence can never carry a Möbius neighbourhood.)*

**Scope — the inertness is a 2-dimensional theorem, not a property of the operation.** It rests on two legs: **Leg A** (separating ⇒ w₁-inert) holds **in every dimension**; **Leg B** (both modes give the *same surface*) rides on **every closed surface admitting an orientation-reversing self-homeomorphism** — which **fails at level-3**, where the seam is an **S²** and the mode **is the mirror choice**, load-bearing on **chiral** summands (`L(5,1)#L(5,1) ≇ L(5,1)#L(5,4)`). **Surfaces are inert; the operation is not.** The mode parameter is therefore **KEPT** (collapsing it would bake a 2-D accident into the API — and the Klein bottle would be unreachable through the general op).

### 7 · THE ACQUISITION CHAIN — how a born form's complex is recovered
`acquireComplex(shape, ancestry)`: **direct → word/collapse replay → cut-derivation → identify-recovery**, applied **recursively over the walked lineage** and finite by construction (a `seen`-set **refuses** a crafted lineage cycle rather than recursing).
- **Representational refusal ≠ topological unsoundness.** The committed bridge (`inkedFormModel`) legitimately refuses self-loops and parallel edge classes; a sewn form carrying parallel classes is **sound** (gate PASS) but **unreadable by the bridge**. The chain **acquires it anyway — without loosening the bridge.** The bar is not lowered to reach the form.
- **The lineage is WALKED, never SWEPT.** `resolveLineage` follows `genealogy.parentShapeId` link by link; it is never handed the store's population. **A root form resolves an EMPTY ancestry — the chain never invents a parent.**

### 8 · THE CLOSURE MUST REACH THE PERSON — the registry unbounding
A promise kept only in the engine is not kept. Per **ADR 0017 Amdt 2** (*the product is the phenomenon, not the proof*), the person meets the **registry**, not the primitive — so the registry provisions the **full ancestry** to every operation. The person now walks generations through the ops they actually fire: **sew → cut → cut → re-sew**, and at depth four the engine says what it is (`{v:4, e:12, f:6}`, χ=−2, w₁=0 — *genus 2*), with `canApply` **TRUE**: the person is **offered the operation, not a refusal**.

### 9 · SCOPE — what survives, what unifies, what does NOT retire
- **The named polygon word-ops SURVIVE** at the **catalogue** — they are the zoo's entry point and the user's vocabulary (removing them would be minimalism; **ADR 0014** forbids it).
- **There is NOTHING to unify — and the general enactment must NOT be made to carry the words.** *(This clause originally ruled the opposite — "the machinery unifies under the general op" — on the premise that two implementations existed and could drift. **WITHDRAWN 2026-07-12: the premise was false.** On the shared single-face domain there has only ever been **one** implementation — the polygon machinery — reached through two doors. Routing the words through the general enactment would **move every born form's vertex ids** (`mat:` → `idn:`), which the recovery **parses** — a real behaviour change bought to remove a duplication that does not exist.)* The two legs stay as they are: **words delegate; complexes enact.**
- **The single-face gate does NOT retire.** A polygon *word* is **slot-addressed to ONE polygon** and **denotes nothing on a complex** — the gate is the word's **honest scope**, not an implementation limit. What was retired is the *lie* (silently dropping faces). Its refusal now **routes** to the general op.

### 10 · The cross-universe orientation-polarity NORM (Sovereign, 2026-07-09)
Orientation is **gauge across components**; the engine canonically gauge-fixes each form, erasing per-universe semantic polarity, so a structurally-*preserving* cross-universe glue of secretly-**opposed** polarities carries no trace. **The mechanism was DECLINED** (not worth the coding runs). Resolved **normatively**: do **not** chain a contradictory argument across universes; the engine **will confuse it**; **if you do, record the opposition by hand.** No engine work — the diagnosis is kept as the *reason* for the norm.

## Consequences
- **The generative promise of ADR 0008 is kept** — in the engine *and* where it is lived. Forms beget forms with no last generation, and every generation is certified by the committed instruments, not by the seal.
- **Open — the multi-parent seam (the last hole).** `resolveLineage` walks **one** parent link; an `assemble`/`connectedSum` child has **two**. A **bridge-refused** connect-sum child therefore cannot yet be acquired. The closure is complete along the single-parent **spine** and open across the multi-parent **join** — which is `connectedSum` itself. The **record** already models multi-parent (the genealogy DAG; the ledger's multi-source pull-back); it is the **walker** that is chain-shaped. Fix = a DAG walk.
- **CLOSED — THE MISSING WITNESS (found and closed 2026-07-12).** `identifyOnComplex` — the enactment running every multi-face sew, every re-sew, the whole closure to depth four — had been **trusted through four builds and never once executed by a test**. The five-word zoo, which both senior offices believed was checking it, **never runs it** (§2). **The remedy is built and co-ratified: the DIFFERENTIAL ORACLE** — an independently-constructed reference quotient (signed union-find + explicit signed-class rewrite), derived from **this ADR's §§3–5 spec**, living in `diagnose-identify-oracle`. It **agrees with the enactment byte-for-byte** (edge classes, vertex classes, signed slots, shape cycles, ledger membership, gate sets) at every generation of the depth-4 chain; it **catches five distinct injected defects** (endpoint-keyed classes · flipped mode convention · path-2 · face cycles unrewritten through `resultOf` · mis-seeded ledger); and it **reproduces the cylinder seal and the depth-4 surface alone**. **Standing instrument — it must remain live and independent.**
  **Two clauses are now enforced structurally, not rhetorically:** (i) **every witnessed case asserts `via === 'general'`**, and the delegating square is asserted a **non-witness counted by nothing** — the 2026-07-12 error is now a permanent check that cannot recur; (ii) the oracle **proves its own trap-sensitivity** — a single uncaught mutation voids it.
- **CLOSED AND BUILT — the mode's reference is the CANONICAL WEDGE (§6.0-bis), committed `ef704d0`.** Found by the differential oracle, which marked the interior pairs UNPINNED and **routed** the question rather than reading the answer out of the implementation — *an echo inherits the resolution and never sees the gap*. The shipped rule turned out to be **worse than unpinned: it was not well-defined** (`wedgeDirections` took the wedge of whichever face came **first in the array**, so `identifyOnComplex` was a function of the complex **AND its array order** — 2 distinct merge partitions over 16 pure relabellings). The canonical wedge kills it; **free-edge behaviour moved by zero bytes**; and **the oracle's interior vertex/sign legs are now UN-RESTRICTED and agree on the full byte-structure — `identifyOnComplex` has no unwitnessed corner left.**
- **Banked:** the **level-3 seam-mode pin** (the S²-seam mirror choice; which of our level-3 forms are chiral; and whether the engine's canonical orientation gauge can even *carry* the distinction — it may collide with the declined polarity mechanism of §10, which would be a finding, not a bug).
- **Method (adopted in this arc, binding on every seat that writes a seal):**
  > **A seal must PROVE its own trap-sensitivity** — it must exhibit, *in the seal*, the wrong mechanism **visibly failing** on the sealed representative. **A seal that the wrong mechanism would also pass is not a seal; it is a coincidence.**
