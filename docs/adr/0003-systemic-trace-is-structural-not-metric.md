# 0003 — The systemic trace is structural, not metric (the bends are secondary)

Ambo emits a **rigid** metric structure (definite angles/distances). A module operation (e.g. edge x—y → circle) breaks that rigidity and forces the rest of the structure to deform — to "bend and break."

Decision: the systemic trace records the **structural** consequence — the combinatorial/topological identifications, merges, and clashes that are forced — and treats the specific metric **bends-and-breaks** as secondary. It is structural in two senses: *not metric* (the deformed geometry is not the deliverable) and *not semantic* (meaning is induced later, downstream — the cardinal law). De-rigidification is the *means* (release the embedding to operate); the structural consequence is the *end*.

## Rejected / secondary

Computing the specific metric bends-and-breaks as the deliverable. They are part of the meaning but secondary — and must **not** be rebuilt as a metric "field" (the carrier-borne field is a closed verdict, absent/scoped). The structural consequence lives in the engine's metric-free data (**lineage + incidence**), never in positions — which is also why it is independent of how the substrate happens to bend.

## Consequence

Resolves the long-flagged "de-rigidification" bridge question: the module *does* break rigidity, but only to read off the structural consequence — not to track the deformed geometry. It also places the module precisely: the purely structural middle layer — metric stripped below, semantics deferred above.
