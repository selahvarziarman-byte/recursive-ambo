WHY IT EXISTS. `resolveLineage` walks `genealogy.parentShapeId` in a while-loop
— ONE link. An assemble / connectedSum child carries `parentShapeId: null`
(multiform.ts: "null BECAUSE IT IS SINGLE-VALUED"). Measured on a
connectedSum(T-squared, T-squared) child:

    resolveLineage(child)            ->  0 ancestors — BOTH PARENTS UNREACHABLE
    buildGenealogyDag([...])         ->  2 parents, 2 edges, integrity accepted
    child.genealogy.sourceVertexIds  ->  32 sites: 16 tracing to A, 16 to B

  THE RECORD IS COMPLETE AND CORRECT. THE WALKER CANNOT REACH IT.
  What is chain-shaped is the WALKER, not the RECORD.

The recovery mechanism is already committed in genealogyDag.ts: a candidate
parent m is a parent of child c iff c's `sourceVertexIds` intersect m's
`createdVertexIds`. Its own comment: "No second parentage model is invented;
both signals are committed ShapeGenealogy fields." And `ancestorsOf(dag, id)` —
the DAG-shaped reachability walk — ALREADY EXISTS and nothing consumes it.

= ancestry[0]), and ManuscriptView.tsx:644 (the ancestry the manuscript DRAWS).
The ratified design language (design ADR 0001) says birth = pencil-pentimento

THE TRAP INSIDE THE FIX — THE CANONICAL-WEDGE BUG, ONE LEVEL UP.
`GenealogyNode.parents` is documented "read forward (INPUT ORDER)". Measured
across 6 permutations of the store array:

    ancestor SET   -> 1  {A,B}          INVARIANT
    ancestor ORDER -> 2  [A,B] / [B,A]  MOVES WITH THE STORE ARRAY ORDER

And the order is load-bearing (playgroundStore:260 takes ancestry[0]; the
manuscript draws parents in order). A DAG-shaped walker that naively inherits
that input-order `parents` list would trade a BLIND walker for an
ARRAY-ORDER-DEPENDENT one — the exact bug ef704d0 just buried. THE ORDER MUST
COME FROM COMMITTED IDENTITY: the child's committed birth id encodes the
argument order (…csum:mpA:…+mpB:… — A before B).

THE SEAL CARRIES FOUR CLAUSES, EACH PROVING ITS OWN TEETH:

  CLAUSE 1 — EXECUTE WHAT YOU WITNESS: every multi-parent case asserts it took

  CLAUSE 2 — TRAP-SENSITIVITY, AND CARRY THE WRONG MECHANISM IN-MEMORY: the

  CLAUSE 3 — ORDER INVARIANCE (new): sweep >= 6 store-array permutations; the
    ancestor order must not move. Teeth: an input-order walker must VISIBLY

  CLAUSE 4 — SINGLE-PARENT NON-MOVEMENT (the highest bar; the analogue of

    single-parent chain is a HARD FAIL: the change is wrong, not the chain.
