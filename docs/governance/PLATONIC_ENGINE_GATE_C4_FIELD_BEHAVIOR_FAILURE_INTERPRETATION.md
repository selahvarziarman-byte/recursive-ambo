## 8. Likely Mechanism Of Failure

The working interpretation is that the current field propagation law deforms source-level phase opposition at probe points.

The field sampler computes each contribution angle in the form:

```txt
angle = source.waveNumber * distance + source.phase
```

A source-level phase opposition:

```txt
phase_j = phase_i + pi
```

does not remain a simple opposition at a probe when the sources have different wave numbers or different distances to that probe:

```txt
phase_j(sample) - phase_i(sample)
  = pi + k_j * d_j(sample) - k_i * d_i(sample)
```

The terms `k_j * d_j(sample)` and `k_i * d_i(sample)` are field-propagation terms. If wave numbers differ, distances differ, or both differ, then source-state opposition can be deformed before the detector sees raw field behavior.

This is the working interpretation, not the final explanation. C.4R records the failure meaning. C.4D must test whether any recoverable source-regime residue remains after geometry and common propagation effects are isolated.

## 9. Geometry-Coded Behavior Warning

Blind-to-position is not the same as geometry-free.

The C.4 detector may avoid direct source-position and probe-position fields, but sampled field contributions can still carry geometry implicitly. The propagation equation depends on distance. Distance depends on the geometry of source and probe placement.

Therefore, if a control recovers structure from raw field behavior, that does not automatically prove source-state success. It may prove that geometry or common propagation effects are encoded in behavior strongly enough to be recovered.

This warning cuts both ways:

```txt
a candidate failure may be caused by deformation through propagation;
a control success may be caused by geometry-coded behavior;
a detector pass may still be diagnostic integrity rather than source-regime success.
```

C.4D must distinguish source-regime residue from geometry/common propagation effects.
