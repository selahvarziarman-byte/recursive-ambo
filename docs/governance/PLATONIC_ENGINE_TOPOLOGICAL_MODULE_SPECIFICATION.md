## 1. Placement in the architecture (the middle layer)

```txt
ambo / dual universe   ->   TOPOLOGICAL MODULE   ->   semantic layer
(produces NAMED              (transforms named         (interprets the
 geometric-combinatorial      material through          consequences of
 material)                    topological operations)   those transformations)
```

The Topological Module is **neither the ambo generator nor the semantic interpreter.** It is the middle layer where named geometry becomes transformable topology. This sharpens and fulfills Ground Plan §4.5 ("semantic-topological workspace… an independent process space… where do names live after topological transformation").

## 2. Purpose and core idea

A **label-preserving topological transformation layer.** It receives named material from the ambo/dual universes — vertices, edges, faces, polyhedra, child-sites, dwellings, concept-labels, inherited relations, selected substructures — and treats that material as a manipulable topological object.

Its purpose is NOT to generate new ambo children and NOT to assign semantic names. Its purpose is to let ambo-derived material undergo topological operations **while preserving a trace of what happens to every named site carried by that material.**

Core idea (the cuboctahedron square): in the ambo/dual universe, a square face of a cuboctahedron is a named geometric-combinatorial object. In the Topological Module that same square can be lifted out as labelled material and subjected to gluing, cutting, identifying, folding, thickening, puncturing, collapsing, cylinder-making, torus-making, or quotienting. Because the square's vertices, edges, face, diagonals, center, concepts, and dwellings may already be named, **every topological operation also creates a traceable transformation of names.**

Most compressed form:

> When named ambo/dual material is topologically transformed, what happens to the names, sites, dwellings, and concepts carried by that material?

## 3. The labelled cell complex model

Treat imported ambo/dual material as a **labelled cell complex.** Vertices are 0-cells, edges are 1-cells, faces are 2-cells, higher cells included when relevant. Each cell may carry names, concepts, dwellings, source-signatures, inherited relations, or other Platonic Engine metadata.

A topological operation transforms this labelled cell complex into another topological object. The essential output is not only the new object, but a **correspondence ledger** from pre-operation material to post-operation material.

## 4. Allowed operations

```txt
gluing · cutting · identifying · folding · quotienting · thickening · puncturing ·
collapsing · subdividing · cylinder construction · torus construction · covering ·
boundary extraction · seam creation
```

## 5. The central artifact — the transformation/correspondence ledger

The important output is NOT merely "a torus" or "a cylinder." It is the before/after ledger recording, for each named item, which cells:

```txt
survived · were identified · were duplicated · became boundaries · became seams ·
collapsed · became loops · reversed orientation · changed adjacency
```

Example phrasing: "this named square became a torus by identifying these opposite edges; these four named vertices are now one vertex-class; these two edge-pairs became loop-classes; these concepts now share a topological site."

## 6. Cardinal discipline — structural event first, semantics induced later

Semantic effects are **induced, not guessed.** The module does not decide that a topological identification "means" a specific concept. It records the structural event first; later semantic interpretation reads from that event. Gluing may induce fusion, equivalence, aliasing, or enforced adjacency — but the module first records the actual topological fact: *these two labelled edges were identified under this operation.*

## 7. Worked example (cuboctahedron square)

```txt
Lift one square face into the module: it arrives with 4 named vertices, 4 named edges,
its face identity, and any attached dwellings/concepts.

Glue ONE pair of opposite edges  -> CYLINDER. Ledger records which vertices were
  identified, which edges became a seam-cycle, which boundaries remain open, how the
  named dwellings changed their topological relations.

Glue BOTH pairs of opposite edges -> TORUS. In the standard square model: all 4 corner
  vertices become ONE vertex-class; the two edge-pairs become two fundamental loop-classes.
  The concepts formerly attached to 4 distinct ambo sites now occupy a shared identified
  topological site. That identification MAY later become semantically meaningful.
```

## 8. Operating requirements (sovereign, verbatim intent)

```txt
1. INPUT — selected material from the ambo/dual universe: a whole polyhedron, a face, a
   path, an edge-cycle, a subcomplex, or a named site configuration.
2. IMPORTED MATERIAL REMAINS NAMED — not anonymous topology. A square is a SPECIFIC square
   from a specific ambo/dual object, carrying its vertices, edges, face-name, source-context,
   and any attached concepts or dwellings.
3. TOPOLOGICAL OPERATIONS ARE ALLOWED — the §4 set.
4. EVERY OPERATION MUST PRODUCE A TRACE — the §5 before/after ledger, per named site.
5. SEMANTIC EFFECTS ARE INDUCED, NOT GUESSED — §6; record the structural event first.
6. THE CENTRAL ARTIFACT IS THE TRANSFORMATION LEDGER — §5; the new object alone is not the point.
```

```txt
You are working on the Topological Module of the Platonic Engine. Assume you already
understand the ambo/dual universes and their named shapes, sites, dwellings, concepts, and
inheritance structures.

The Topological Module begins when selected named material from the ambo/dual universe is
imported as a labelled topological object (a vertex, edge, face, path, cycle, polyhedron, or
larger subcomplex). Once imported, the material may undergo topological operations: gluing,
cutting, identifying, folding, puncturing, collapsing, thickening, quotienting, cylinder
construction, torus construction, covering, boundary extraction, and related transformations.

Your task is to preserve a COMPLETE CORRESPONDENCE between the original ambo/dual material and
the transformed topological object. Track what happens to every named site, edge, face,
dwelling, and concept. Record whether each item survives, merges, duplicates, becomes a
boundary, becomes a seam, collapses, reverses orientation, enters a loop, or changes adjacency.

Do NOT prematurely assign poetic or semantic meanings. First produce the topological event
ledger. Semantic interpretation may later read from that ledger.

Core question: when named ambo/dual material is transformed topologically, what happens to the
names and dwellings carried by that material?
```
