# 0001 — The inked-manuscript language (world as paper, forms as drawings)

Status: ACCEPTED — ratified by the Mothership (4th seating), 2026-07-08
Date: 2026-07-08 · Seat: designer

## Context
The target is a thinking space (a prosthesis for thinking). The initiation rejects the neon "sci-fi
math" costume and the generic-minimal template alike, and demands §2 faithfulness. The sovereign
first described the workspace as a living **ocean / biosphere** stratified by dimension; we
prototyped it as deep-water with matte-ceramic form bodies. Two problems surfaced: (a) the ceramic
look was never dialled and needs a heavy renderer (subsurface/refraction); (b) an inked-drawing body
and a deep-dark ground are tonally incompatible — dark ink vanishes on dark water.

## Decision
Adopt **one idiom for the whole surface: ink & graphite on warm paper.**
- Forms are **inked drawings** — the real subdivision as construction lines, an ink silhouette, the
  real generators in colour — not photoreal bodies.
- The world is a **living manuscript** (the biosphere, drawn): forms born, drifting, coexisting,
  dimension-stratified as tonal registers; the genealogy as marginalia / the record.
- The chrome (dock, specimen cards) is already this idiom, so chrome + world + forms unify.

## Consequences
+ Full unification and a real point of view (not the rejected defaults).
+ Honest: a drawing reads openly as a chosen representative; the construction lines ARE the real complex.
+ Renderable without subsurface/refraction (line work + hatching) → achievable in R3F.
− Gives up the deep-water atmosphere the ocean had.
− Non-photoreal rendering has its own craft to nail (silhouette quality, hatching, avoiding "flat/dead").
− Prior "ocean" language reframes to "manuscript" (the living-forms qualities are retained).

## Alternatives considered
- Deep-water ocean + matte/glass bodies — rejected: tonal clash with inked bodies, heavy to render, less unified.
- Ink only on select (soft world, inked specimen) — rejected: world not inked, two rendering styles.
