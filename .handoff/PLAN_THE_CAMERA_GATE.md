## §2 · THE ACT VOCABULARY — candidates recorded for the designer's ruling (her brief was `1615` + `B-0b`, both parked with this file)
1. ★ **DOOR-ADDRESSED CROSSING** — a key per door: press it, cross it. **A walk becomes a pressed WORD** (`a · b · a⁻¹ · b⁻¹` = four presses). Consequences recorded when it was live: returns the commutator experiment to HUMAN hands (softens Δ59 — the visual thicket was the inhuman part, not the pattern); the drive family's walking legs drive by key events (E.circuit's recut gets cheaper); **and the route DISPLAYED as the word walked is half of B2's order-reading surface.** Open design questions: how doors wear their addresses in-room (the gluing word's letters are the ratified vocabulary) · how many doors a hand addresses (T³ 3 pairs, SW 6) · inverse crossings (shift+key? the paired face's own key?).
2. ★ **ORBIT-ABOUT-A-TARGET** (Δ62) — a chosen shape or edge as PIVOT; the walker circles it while standing off it; full 360°. Open question: how the pivot is picked from inside a room (the selected thing? the nearest? addressed like a door?).
3. **ORIENTATION SNAPS** — face the nearest door squarely · level the horizon (hyperbolic drift makes this a real act) · return to entry orientation.
4. **COUNTED STEPS** — one press = one clean step, for counted paths, beside hold-to-glide.
5. **360° FREE LOOK standing in place** — keys turn the view fully, no hold (the phase-1 class act; parked with the rest by Δ63).
