**The question the readers were asked** (README): in the practice as done, what does the third pole (the vertex opposite the edge) DO at the midpoint: to the midpoint's content, to its name, to which items meet which?

**Words used here, in plain terms.**
- *Pole / vertex*: one of the three concepts at the corners of a triangle. *Edge*: a pair of them. *Midpoint / edge-site*: the new concept named between the pair. *The third / the opposite / the excluded*: the corner not on that edge.
- *Gloss*: the sentence saying what a concept means at its place. *Handle*: the word chosen for it. *Representative*: handle plus gloss, standing for a richer region, never the region's essence.
- *Carrier / carrier-view*: a triangle the edge sits in, read from that triangle's opposite corner. One edge can sit in two triangles, so one site can have two carrier-views.
- *Generation / descent*: naming the three midpoints of a triangle makes four smaller triangles; naming their fresh edges is the next generation. In a corner triangle two edges are fresh and one is inherited (it already has a midpoint, from the parent).
- *Nearest wrong neighbour*: the best candidate that must be rejected, and why, stated beside the winner.
- *Foot (orthogonal foot)*: the point on an edge where the perpendicular dropped from the opposite corner lands. On an equilateral triangle it is the edge's midpoint.
- *Abduction / backpropagation*: inferring a missing corner from the names already given along an edge.
- In the engine (ADR 0031): a *cast* is a concept's roles with their relation-types; *J* is the person's pairing of roles across an edge; *M = A ⊔_J B* is the midpoint, the two casts laid side by side with the paired roles identified (a pushout); the *face reading* Fix · Mov · Und is what the third's corner sees of that gluing; the *feet* and *respects* are records made through the third.
