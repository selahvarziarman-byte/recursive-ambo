# Run 2 · the four casts and their sources

Mark on every role: `world` ∈ objective · subjective · social · symbolic (Habermas's three worlds, TCA I ch. 1, plus the symbolic order). Shared words in all four casts: `grounds`, `independent-of`. Records are three-valued; absence is absence.

## Fact (corner A)
| role | world | source |
|---|---|---|
| F1 State of affairs | objective | Husserl, Logical Investigations (Sachverhalt); Wittgenstein, Tractatus 2–2.01 |
| F2 Truth-maker | objective | Russell, Philosophy of Logical Atomism (1918); Armstrong, A World of States of Affairs (1997) |
| F3 Social fact | social | Durkheim, Rules of Sociological Method (1895); Searle, Construction of Social Reality (1995) |
| F4 Facticity | subjective | Heidegger, Being and Time §29; Sartre, Being and Nothingness II.1 |
| F5 Constituted fact | symbolic | Fleck, Genesis and Development of a Scientific Fact (1935); Latour & Woolgar, Laboratory Life (1979) |

Words: counts-as (Searle), constituted-by (Durkheim/Fleck), thrown-into (Heidegger), grounds, independent-of. Record: a state of affairs counts as a social fact and grounds it, not the reverse; social and constituted facts constitute each other; facticity is thrown into states of affairs and social facts; states of affairs are independent of their constitution, social facts are not; facticity is not independent of what is the case.

## Value (corner B)
| role | world | source |
|---|---|---|
| V1 Good in itself | objective | Aristotle, NE I.7; Moore, Principia Ethica (1903) |
| V2 Instrumental value | objective | Dewey, Theory of Valuation (1939); Marx, Capital I.1 (use value) |
| V3 Value-positing | subjective | Nietzsche, Genealogy of Morals; Heidegger, "Nietzsche's Word: God is Dead" |
| V4 Fittingness | subjective | Brentano (1889); Scheler, Formalism (1913); Scanlon (1998) |
| V5 Validity claim | social | Weber, "Science as a Vocation" (1919); Habermas, TCA (1981); Kant's ought |

Words: for-the-sake-of (Aristotle), posits (Nietzsche), merits (Brentano), binds (Kant/Habermas), grounds, independent-of. Record: the instrumental is for the sake of the good; positing posits the good (the continental lean, against Moore); the good merits the fitting attitude and grounds it; validity binds positing and is independent of it; the good is not independent of positing (Nietzsche's claim kept as the record's).

## Meaning (corner C)
| role | world | source |
|---|---|---|
| M1 Sense | symbolic | Frege, "On Sense and Reference" (1892); Husserl, Ideas I |
| M2 Reference | objective | Frege (1892); Russell, "On Denoting" (1905) |
| M3 Use | social | Wittgenstein, PI §43; Austin, How to Do Things with Words (1962) |
| M4 Différance | symbolic | Saussure, Course (1916); Derrida, Of Grammatology (1967), "Différance" (1968) |
| M5 Significance | subjective | Dilthey; Heidegger, Being and Time §32; Gadamer, Truth and Method (1960) |

Words: determines (Frege), constituted-by (Wittgenstein), defers (Derrida), projects (Heidegger), grounds, independent-of. Record: sense determines reference; difference determines sense; use does not determine reference; sense is constituted by use, reference is not; différance defers reference and significance; significance is the upon-which of sense and grounds use; reference does not ground sense; reference is independent of use; sense is not independent of difference; significance is independent of reference.

## Action (corner D)
| role | world | source |
|---|---|---|
| A1 Intention under a description | subjective | Anscombe, Intention (1957) |
| A2 Reason as cause | subjective | Davidson, "Actions, Reasons, and Causes" (1963) |
| A3 Deed as beginning | objective | Arendt, The Human Condition (1958); Nietzsche, GM I §13; Hegel, PR §§115–118 |
| A4 Communicative act | social | Austin (1962); Habermas, TCA (1981) |
| A5 Imputation | social | Kant, Metaphysics of Morals 6:227; Ricoeur, Oneself as Another (1990) |

Words: rationalizes, causes (Davidson), under-description (Anscombe), begins (Arendt), answers-for (Ricoeur), grounds, independent-of. Record: the reason rationalizes and grounds the intention and causes the deed; the intention does not cause the deed (Anscombe against Davidson, kept as the record's); the deed is under the description of the intention and of the imputation; the deed begins the communicative sequence; imputation answers for the deed, not for the reason; communication grounds imputation; the deed does not ground its reason; the deed is not independent of its reason; the communicative act not of the intention; imputation not of communication.
