A mock on Virgin Land's Culture: the child's relations as filled loops, the naming of its roles, and zoom

Every role, word and count is read from her 19:34 export (ADR 0031 §9.40–§9.41, ratified). Nothing is saved: a reload clears what you say here. Nothing is proposed: every choice starts unchosen and every field opens empty.

Try

  • Choose a role. Its card lists its closed loops, one at a time. A loop between two roles is closed when both parents say something there (the same role counts). An open loop, where one parent is silent, is never shown.
  • Each loop is drawn once. Its parents' relations run along the top and bottom, and the two relatings (the child's two roles) are the uprights. Its two diagonals are the two questions it asks. Each question has two ways round: one goes by Value's side, the other by Fact's.
  • Say what each way comes to, as in the modes tab. Type the word, and your own words that begin with what you typed are offered under the field. A word you have not used before is marked · a new word. Then decide, or press comes to nothing. If both ways come to the same, the loop is filled: a relation of the child, drawn as an arc you can name. If they differ, it is a hole, shown and never resolved. If you have not said, it waits.
  • Say it once for every loop of a shape: say it for: every loop of this shape, with or without the modes too. The count shows what it binds before you say anything.
  • Loops across a refusal are form. They are counted apart and listed on show, and you cannot fill them.
  • Hover an arc in the drawing to read what it is.
  • Name a role (name it), then try the same name on another role. The second is refused.
  • Zoom: whole · zoom in · zoom out, or ctrl + wheel (a pinch) over the drawing. Once zoomed in, drag to move it. The drawing never changes size by itself.

On Culture, from the file

    Ruled since Arman's look (§9.42–§9.44)

    • Both diagonals are asked. A loop is asked twice, from i's Value role to j's Fact role and from j's to i's. Each has its own two ways round. Pointing at a question lights its diagonal in the drawing.
    • One diagonal agreeing and the other coming to nothing is filled. Different answers on either make a hole. Nothing on every way is settled, no relation.
    • On a three-sided loop, one way at each diagonal is the relating itself. If the other comes to nothing, that is a refused route: shown, and it fills nothing.
    • Both ways coming to a word barred at that pair is a tension: shown, and it fills nothing.
    • Two words at one pair (a two-sided loop) are not asked. They are counted apart and listed on show.
    • A loop's own answer stands before a rule's; where they differ, it reads as an exception to the rule.
